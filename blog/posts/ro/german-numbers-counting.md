# German Numbers: Complete Guide from 0 to 1 Billion

You're learning German. You go shopping. The cashier says: "Das macht vierundzwanzig Euro und fünfzig Cent."

Your reaction: "Wait... how much was that?"

Numbers are essential for everyday life in Germany. Shopping, telling time, addresses, phone numbers — you need numbers everywhere!

**The good news:** German numbers follow clear patterns. Once you learn 1-12, you can count to billions!

## Ordinal Numbers (First, Second, Third...)

Ordinal numbers indicate order or position:

| Cardinal | Ordinal | Meaning |
|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | **Erste** | first |
| 2 | **Zweite** | second |
| 3 | **Dritte** | third |
| 4 | **Vierte** | fourth |
| 5 | **Fünfte** | fifth |
| 6 | **Sechste** | sixth |
| 7 | **Siebte** | seventh |
| 8 | **Achte** | eighth |
| 9 | **Neunte** | ninth |
| 10 | **Zehnte** | tenth |
| 11 | **Elfte** | eleventh |
| 12 | **Zwölfte** | twelfth |
| 13 | **Dreizehnte** | thirteenth |
| 20 | **Zwanzigste** | twentieth |
| 21 | **Einundzwanzigste** | twenty-first |
| 100 | **Hundertste** | hundredth |
| 1000 | **Tausendste** | thousandth |

**Formation rules:**

1. Add **-te** for numbers 1-19
   - drei → drit**te**
   - acht → ach**te**
   
2. Add **-ste** for numbers 20+
   - zwanzig → zwanzig**ste**
   - hundert → hundert**ste**

3. Compound numbers: Add ending to the LAST number
   - einundzwanzig → einundzwanzig**ste** (21st)

4. Adjective endings apply
   - der erste Tag
   - das zweite Haus
   - die dritte Frau

## Using Numbers in Sentences

### Simple Counting

- Ich habe **zwei** Katzen. (I have two cats)
- Er ist **sechzehn** Jahre alt. (He is sixteen years old)
- Wir trinken **fünf** Bier. (We drink five beers)

### Telling Time

- Es ist **acht** Uhr. (It's eight o'clock)
- Ich komme um **siebzehn** Uhr. (I come at seventeen hundred / 5 PM)
- Der Zug fährt um **vierundzwanzig** Uhr ab. (The train departs at twenty-four hundred / 4 PM)

### Shopping and Prices

- Das kostet **zwanzig** Euro. (That costs twenty euros)
- Ich möchte **drei** Kilo Äpfel. (I'd like three kilos of apples)
- Das macht **siebenundfünfzig** Euro. (That makes seventy-five euros)

### Phone Numbers

- Meine Nummer ist **null-zwei-zwei-null-null-acht**. (My number is 02202008)
- Rufen Sie mich unter **null-drei-zwo** an. (Call me at 032)

### Addresses

- Ich wohne in der **Müllerstraße sieben**. (I live at Müller Street seven)
- Postleitzahl **acht null fünf** (postal code 8005)

### Dates and Years

- Heute ist der **erste** Mai. (Today is the first of May)
- Im Jahr **zweitausend** (in the year two thousand)
- Meine Geburtsjahr ist **neunzehnhundertachtundachtzig**. (1988)

## Special Number Expressions

### Common Phrases

- **Null** problems = Keine Probleme
- **Number one** = Die Nummer eins
- **A million times** = Eine Million Mal
- **One moment, please** = Einen Moment bitte
- **Once upon a time** = Es war einmal

### German Idioms with Numbers

- **Ein** mal **Kein** Mal = Once in a blue moon (literally: once never)
- **Alle** guten Dinge sind **drei** = All good things come in threes
- **Teufel** ist **sechs** = The devil is at six (to be in trouble)
- **unter** **allen** Umständen = at all costs (literally: under all circumstances)

## Pronunciation Tips

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. "Eins" vs "Ein"**

- Use **Eins** when counting alone: "Es ist eins."
- Use **Ein** before a noun: "Ein Haus."

**2. "Drei" vs "Dreiunddreissig"**

- Don't say "dreißig" for 33 — it's dreiunddreißig!

**3. Ordinal endings**

- Remember: -te (1-19), -ste (20+)
- Adjective endings apply: der erste, das erste, die erste

**4. Large numbers**

- Germans write numbers differently: 1.000,5 (not 1,000.5)
- Say "Komma" for decimal point: 3,14 = drei Komma eins vier

## Cultural Notes

### Number Superstitions

- **13** (Dreizehn) is considered unlucky in Germany too
- **Friday the 13th** = Unglückstag
- Knocking on wood = **auf Holz klopfen**

### Number Gestures

- **1** = Index finger
- **2** = Index + middle finger
- **3** = Index, middle, ring finger
- **4** = All fingers except thumb
- **5** = Whole hand

### "German Efficiency"

German numbers show the German love of precision:
- Exact prices: "Dreiundfünfzig Euro und zwanzig Cent"
- Exact times: "Sechzehn Uhr siebenunddreissig"
- No rounding until payment!

## Conclusion

German numbers follow clear, logical patterns:

1. **Learn 1-12 first** — these are your building blocks
2. **Teens are easy** — just add "-zehn"
3. **Tens have patterns** -zig ending
4. **21-99** use units + und + tens (one-and-twenty)
5. **100+** are straightforward — hundert, tausend, million
6. **Ordinals** add -te (1-19) or -ste (20+)
7. **Practice daily** — use numbers when shopping, telling time, addresses

**Pro tip:** Start by practicing numbers 1-20 until you can say them without thinking. Then add the tens (20, 30, 40...). After that, the 21-99 pattern becomes automatic.

Remember: German numbers might seem long, but they're completely logical. Once you understand the system, you can count to billions!
