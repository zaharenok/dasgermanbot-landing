# German Slang and Youth Language: 50+ Modern Terms Germans Actually Use

You learned German from textbooks, but when you talk to real Germans — especially young people — they sound like they're speaking a completely different language.

Words like **geil**, **krass**, **alter**... these aren't in your textbook, but Germans use them EVERY DAY.

Ready to sound like a modern German instead of a walking dictionary from 1950? Let's dive into the world of German slang!

## Why Learn German Slang?

**Textbook German** vs **Real German:**
- Textbook: "Das ist sehr gut" (That is very good)
- Real German: "Das ist krass geil!" (That's insanely awesome!)

German slang isn't just "cool" — it's how people actually communicate. If you want to:
- Connect with young Germans
- Understand German social media
- Sound natural and current
- Not sound like a robot

Then you NEED this guide.

## The Essential German Slang Words

### Positive/Agreement Terms

#### 1. Geil

**Meaning:** Awesome, cool, great
**Usage:** Extremely common! Can be used for almost anything positive.
**Examples:**
- "Das Auto ist so geil!" (That car is so awesome!)
- "Geile Party!" (Awesome party!)
- "Das find ich geil" (I think that's great)

**Note:** Originally meant "horny" but has evolved to mean "awesome." Still slightly edgy in formal settings.

#### 2. Krass

**Meaning:** Crazy, insane, intense, awesome (depending on context)
**Usage:** Can be positive OR negative.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist krass!" (That's insane/awesome!)
- "Krasses Wetter" (Crazy weather)
- "Das ist krass schlecht" (That's horribly bad)

#### 3. Hammer

**Meaning:** Amazing, excellent
**Usage:** Positive, enthusiastic.
**Examples:**
- "Der Film war der Hammer!" (The movie was amazing!)
- "Hammer Idee!" (Excellent idea!)

#### 4. Weltklasse

**Meaning:** World-class, excellent
**Usage:** Very positive.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist weltklasse!" (That's world-class!)
- "Ein weltklasses Ergebnis" (A world-class result)

#### 5. Top

**Meaning:** Great, excellent, cool
**Usage:** Positive, casual.
**Examples:**
- "Das war echt top" (That was really great)
- "Top gefahren" (Driving great/well)

### Negative/Critical Terms

#### 6. Cringe

**Meaning:** Cringeworthy, embarrassing, awkward
**Usage:** Direct loan from English.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist so cringe" (That's so cringeworthy)
- "Cringe Situation"

#### 7. Ladend

**Meaning:** Lame, boring, weak
**Usage:** Very common among young people.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist total ladend" (That's totally lame)
- "Ladende Ausrede" (Lame excuse)

#### 8. Käse

**Meaning:** Cheesy, corny, try-hard
**Usage:** For something trying too hard to be cool.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist voll käse" (That's super cheesy)
- "Käsespruche" (Cheesy pick-up lines)

#### 9. Fragwürdig

**Meaning:** Sketchy, suspicious, dodgy
**Usage:** For something/someone untrustworthy.
**Examples:**
- "Der Typ ist voll fragwürdig" (That guy is super sketchy)
- "Fragwürdige Sache"

#### 10. Shitty

**Meaning:** Shitty, terrible
**Usage:** Direct loan from English.
**Examples:**
- "Shitty Wetter" (Shitty weather)
- "Das Essen war shitty" (The food was shitty)

## Address and Referring to People

#### 11. Alter

**Meaning:** Dude, man, bro
**Usage:** Very common way to address friends.
**Examples:**
- "Alter, was geht?" (Dude, what's up?)
- "Ja, klar, Alter" (Yeah, sure, dude)

#### 12. Alte

**Meaning:** Bro, man, friend
**Usage:** Friendly, informal address.
**Examples:**
- "Na, Alte?" (Hey, bro?)
- "Alte, pass auf" (Bro, watch out)

#### 13. Digga

**Meaning:** Bro, homie (from "Dicker" = fatty)
**Usage:** Originally from Rhine region, now nationwide.
**Examples:**
- "Was geht, Digga?" (What's up, bro?)
- "Ja, Digga!"

#### 14. Mei/Oida

**Meaning:** Dude, man (Bavarian/Austrian)
**Usage:** Regional but widely understood.
**Examples:**
- "Oida, was machst du?" (Dude, what are you doing?)
- "Mei, das ist krass!"

#### 15. Frau/Mann (as casual address)

**Meaning:** Miss/Sir (but casual)
**Usage:** Polite but not formal.
**Examples:**
- "Entschuldigung, Frau" (Sorry, miss)
- "Ja, mann" (Yeah, man)

## Agreement and Understanding

#### 16. Genau

**Meaning:** Exactly, that's right
**Usage:** Strong agreement.
**Examples:**
- "Genau!" (Exactly!)
- "Das meine ich auch" (That's what I mean too)

#### 17. Auf jeden Fall

**Meaning:** In any case, anyway, at least
**Usage:** Very common filler.
**Examples:**
- "Auf jeden Fall, wir sehen uns" (Anyway, we'll see each other)
- "Das ist auf jeden Fall gut" (That's definitely good)

#### 18. Klar

**Meaning:** Clear, sure, of course
**Usage:** Agreement.
**Examples:**
- "Klar, das mache ich" (Sure, I'll do it)
- "Das ist klar" (That's clear)

#### 19. Absolut

**Meaning:** Absolutely
**Usage:** Strong agreement.
**Examples:**
- "Absolut!" (Absolutely!)
- "Das stimmt absolut" (That's absolutely right)

#### 20. Eher

**Meaning: Rather
**Usage:** Preference.
**Examples:**
- "Ich würde eher gehen" (I'd rather go)
- "Eher nicht" (Rather not)

## Emphasis and Intensity

#### 21. Voll

**Meaning:** Totally, completely, super
**Usage:** Intensifier.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist voll geil" (That's totally awesome)
- "Ich bin voll müde" (I'm super tired)

#### 22. Echt

**Meaning:** Really, truly, genuinely
**Usage:** Emphasizing truth.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist echt krass" (That's really insane)
- "Ich meine das ernst" (I mean that seriously)

#### 23. Total

**Meaning:** Totally, completely
**Usage:** Direct loan from English.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist total gut" (That's totally good)
- "Total begeistert" (Totally excited)

#### 24. Mega

**Meaning:** Mega, very, extremely
**Usage:** Intensifier.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist mega wichtig" (That's mega important)
- "Megacool" (Megacool)

#### 25. Super

**Meaning:** Super, very
**Usage:** Very common positive.
**Examples:**
- "Superclever" (Supersmart)
- "Supergeil" (Superawesome)

## Action and Movement

#### 26. Abgehen

**Meaning:** To leave, to go (literally: to go off)
**Usage:** Casual way to say goodbye or leave.
**Examples:**
- "Ich muss abgehen" (I have to go)
- "Gehen wir ab?" (Shall we go?)

#### 27. Chillen

**Meaning:** To chill, relax, calm down
**Usage:** Direct loan from English.
**Examples:**
- "Chill mal" (Chill out)
- "Ich will chillen" (I want to chill)

#### 28. Checken

**Meaning:** To check, to verify
**Usage:** Direct loan from English.
**Examples:**
- "Ich muss das checken" (I have to check that)
- "Check mal meine DM" (Check my DM)

#### 29. Feiern

**Meaning:** To celebrate, to party
**Usage:** Having fun, celebrating.
**Examples:**
- "Lass uns feiern!" (Let's celebrate!)
- "Wir feiern heute" (We're celebrating today)

#### 30. Hakeln

**Meaning:** To flirt, to hit on someone
**Usage:** Romantic pursuit.
**Examples:**
- "Er hakelt sie an" (He's hitting on her)
- "Hakeln ist nicht meine Stärke" (Flirting isn't my strength)

## Age-Specific Slang

### For Teenagers/Young Adults

#### 51. Boot

**Meaning:** Boat, but can mean "awesome" or "let's go"
**Usage:** Very flexible youth slang.
**Examples:**
- "Boot, das ist krass" (Wow, that's insane)
- "Boot, wir gehen!" (Let's go!)

#### 52. Fitti

**Meaning:** Forget it, never mind
**Usage:** Dismissing something.
**Examples:**
- "Fitti, das ist egal" (Forget it, that doesn't matter)
- "Fitti, mach weiter" (Never mind, continue)

#### 53. Babo

**Meaning:** Dummy, idiot
**Usage:** Teasing but not very offensive.
**Examples:**
- "Du Babo" (You dummy)
- "Babo sein" (Be a dummy)

### For Young Adults (20s-30s)

#### 54. Ehrenmann/Ehrenfrau

**Meaning:** Literally "honor man/honor woman" = someone who does something impressively
**Usage:** Sarcastic praise.
**Examples:**
- "Der Ehrenmann hat es geschafft" (The hero managed to do it - sarcastic)
- "Ehrenmann sein" (To be a hero)

#### 55. Flexen

**Meaning:** To show off, to flex
**Usage:** Showing off.
**Examples:**
- "Er flext mit seinem Auto" (He's showing off with his car)
- "Kein Grund zu flexen" (No reason to show off)

#### 56. Simpel

**Meaning:** Simply, basically
**Usage:** Emphasizing simplicity.
**Examples:**
- "Das ist simpel gut" (That's simply good)
- "Simpel erklärt" (Simply explained)

### Regional Youth Slang

#### 57. (Northern Germany) Am Damp sein

**Meaning:** To be confused/clueless
**Usage:** "On the hemp" = confused.
**Examples:**
- "Ich bin total am Damp" (I'm totally confused)

#### 58. (Southern Germany) Aller

**Meaning:** Guy/Everyone (Bavarian)
**Usage:** Very common in Bavaria.
**Examples:**
- "Hallo aller!" (Hello everyone!)
- "Was geht ab, aller?" (What's up, guys?)

#### 59. (Berlin) Gib mir mal die Flasche

**Meaning:** Give me the bottle (very Berliner)
**Usage:** Can mean "relax" or "shut up" depending on tone.
**Examples:**
- "Gib mir mal die Flasche, Alter" (Relax, dude)

#### 60. (Rheinland) Bekommen

**Meaning:** To understand, to get it
**Usage:** Understanding something.
**Examples:**
- "Bekommst du das?" (Do you get it?)
- "Ich bekomme das nicht" (I don't get it)

## Modern Phrases and Expressions

#### 61. "Was geht ab?"

**Meaning:** What's up? / What's happening?
**Usage:** Casual greeting.
**Examples:**
- "Na, was geht ab?" (Hey, what's up?)
- "Was geht heute ab?" (What's happening today?)

#### 62. "Kein Bock"

**Meaning:** No desire / Not in the mood
**Usage:** From "Bock" (ram) = male goat.
**Examples:**
- "Ich hab keinen Bock darauf" (I'm not in the mood)
- "Kein Bock, das zu machen" (No desire to do that)

#### 63. "Juckt"

**Meaning:** That rocks / That's great
**Usage:** Expressing approval.
**Examples:**
- "Das juckt!" (That rocks!)
- "Juckt dir?" (Do you like that?)

#### 64. "Passt schon"

**Meaning:** It fits / It works / No problem
**Usage:** Agreement or reassurance.
**Examples:**
- "Das passt schon" (That'll work)
- "Passt schon, keine Sorge" (It's fine, no worries)

#### 65. "Alles gut"

**Meaning:** All good / Everything's fine
**Usage:** Reassuring.
**Examples:**
- "Alles gut bei dir?" (Everything good with you?)
- "Hier ist alles gut" (Everything's fine here)

## Cultural Notes

### German Slang is Diverse

German slang varies by:
- **Age group** (teens vs 20s vs 30s+)
- **Region** (North vs South vs East vs West)
- **City** (Berlin vs Hamburg vs Munich)
- **Context** (party vs work vs online)

### Slang Evolves Fast

Words that were cool 5 years ago might be "cringe" today:
- "Yolo" - already dated
- "Swag" - definitely cringe
- "Frickeln" - replaced by more modern terms

### Classic Slang That Endures

Some slang has stood the test of time:
- **Geil** (since the 70s, still going strong)
- **Krass** (versatile, still popular)
- **Alter** (timeless way to address friends)
