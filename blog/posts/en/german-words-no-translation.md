# 15 German Words That Don't Exist in Any Other Language

Every language has gaps. English has no word for the day after tomorrow. Japanese has no word for "no." But German? German has words for feelings you didn't even know you had.

These aren't just long compound nouns (although some are). These are concepts so specific, so perfectly captured, that other languages just... gave up and borrowed the German word instead. Here are 15 of the best.

## 1. Schadenfreude

The pleasure you feel when someone else fails. You know the feeling. A coworker who always brags spills coffee on their white shirt. That tiny moment of joy? That's *Schadenfreude* — literally "damage-joy." English doesn't have a word for it. Neither does anyone else. They just use the German one.

## 2. Fernweh

The opposite of homesickness. It's the ache to be somewhere far away. *Fern* (far) + *Weh* (pain). You're sitting at your desk, staring at a photo of Patagonia, and your chest hurts. That's *Fernweh*.

## 3. Torschlusspanik

Literally "gate-closing panic." The fear that time is running out — for a relationship, a career, having kids, life in general. Originally about medieval cities closing their gates at night. Now it's what you feel at 3 AM when you realize you're 35 and haven't learned to cook.

## 4. Verschlimmbessern

To make something worse while trying to improve it. *Verschlimmern* (worsen) + *verbessern* (improve). You try to fix a typo in a presentation and accidentally delete the whole slide. You just *verschlimmbessert* it.

## 5. Weltschmerz

"World-pain." The existential sadness you feel when reality doesn't match your ideals. You read the news and feel a deep, philosophical exhaustion. That's *Weltschmerz*. German poets invented this in the 1830s. It's only gotten more relevant.

## 6. Fremdschämen

Second-hand embarrassment. Watching someone bomb a toast at a wedding and cringing so hard your spine hurts. *Fremd* (foreign/other) + *schämen* (to be ashamed). You're embarrassed on behalf of someone who isn't embarrassed at all.

## 7. Backpfeifengesicht

A face that desperately needs to be slapped. *Backpfeife* (slap) + *Gesicht* (face). Not that you would slap it. Just that the face seems to invite it. Use this word carefully.

## 8. Kummerspeck

"Grief-bacon." The weight you gain from emotional overeating. After a breakup, you eat pizza for a week and your jeans don't fit anymore? That extra weight has a name: *Kummerspeck*. German doesn't judge. It just describes.

## 9. Sitzfleisch

"Sitting meat." The ability to endure something boring for a long time without giving up. Used for people who sit through 4-hour meetings, finish terrible books, or never leave the casino. It's a compliment. Sort of.

## 10. Treppenwitz

"Staircase joke." The perfect comeback you think of when you're already leaving — walking down the stairs. The French call it *l'esprit de l'escalier*, but the Germans made it a single compound noun because of course they did.

## 11. Vorfreude

The joy you feel *before* something good happens. Not during. Before. You booked a trip to Japan and it's three months away, but you're already happy just thinking about it. That's *Vorfreude* — "pre-joy." Research shows it's often more intense than the event itself.

## 12. Schnapsidee

A "booze idea." An idea so stupid it could only have come from someone drunk. Your friend suggests quitting their job to become a professional moonwalker. That's a *Schnapsidee*.

## 13. Feierabend

The moment you finish work for the day. Not "after work." Not "clocking out." *Feierabend* is a celebration. *Feier* (celebration) + *Abend* (evening). Germans treat the end of the workday like a minor holiday. And honestly? They're right.

## 14. Herzschmerz

"Heart-pain." Not just heartbreak after a relationship ends. It's the broader ache of longing, nostalgia, or unrequited love. More poetic than "heartbreak." More specific than "sadness."

## 15. Ohrwurm

"Ear worm." That song stuck in your head on repeat. You heard "Last Christmas" in a store in July and now it's been playing in your brain for six hours. You have an *Ohrwurm*. There's no cure. Only time.
