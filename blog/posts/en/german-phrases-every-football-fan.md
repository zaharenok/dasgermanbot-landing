# German Phrases Every Football Fan Needs To Know

Football in Germany is not a sport. It is a religion. The Bundesliga fills stadiums of 80,000 people every weekend. The national team draws the entire country to TV screens. And the commentary? Pure German poetry. If you watch a match in Germany, in a bar, with Germans, you need to understand what they are screaming about. Here is every essential German football phrase, ranked by how often you will hear it.

## 1. «Tor!» — The only word that matters

Goal. You will hear this screamed, roared, whispered, and sobbed. A single syllable that contains an entire emotional journey. When Germany scores, the entire bar erupts: «TOOOR!» The longer the vowel, the better the goal. A last-minute equalizer gets a five-second «TOOOOOOOOR!» You don't need German for anything else. Just this word.

## 2. «Schiri, are you blind?!»

«Schiri» is short for Schiedsrichter — the referee. Every German fan has screamed this at least once. The referee is always wrong. Every call is questionable. «Schiri, bist du blind?!» (Ref, are you blind?!) is the national anthem of every stadium. Learn it. You will use it.

## 3. «Abseits» — Offside

The most debated word in football, and Germans debate it passionately. «Das war doch gar kein Abseits!» (That wasn't offside at all!) Every fan in the bar is suddenly a VAR expert. The word itself is simple. The arguments it causes last entire evenings.

## 4. «Elfmeter» — Penalty kick

Literally «eleven meters.» The distance from the penalty spot to the goal line. When the referee points to the spot, the entire room holds its breath. «Elfmeter!» someone whispers. Then everyone has an opinion. Was it a foul? Was it a dive? Should VAR check it? The debate never ends.

## 5. «Schwalbe» — The dive

Literally «swallow» — the bird. Germans call diving a «swallow» because the player falls like a bird tumbling from the sky. «Das war eine Schwalbe!» (That was a dive!) is screamed every time a player touches the grass. Beautiful metaphor. Dirty tactic. German language wins again.

## 6. «Freistoß» — Free kick

«Free kick.» You will hear this announced by the commentator and immediately debated by everyone in the room. Direct or indirect? Should it be a card? Was the foul even real? The free kick itself is almost secondary to the discussion it generates.

## 7. «Ecke» — Corner kick

Short for Eckball. When the commentator yells «Ecke für Deutschland!» the energy shifts. Corners mean opportunity. Corners mean hope. The entire bar leans forward. «Ecke!» is one syllable of pure anticipation.

## 8. «Anpfiff» and «Abpfiff» — Kickoff and final whistle

«Anpfiff» = starting whistle. «Abpfiff» = final whistle. The bookends of every match. Before Anpfiff: nervous energy, predictions, hope. After Abpfiff: either euphoria or silence. «Und Abpfiff! Das Spiel ist aus!» (And the final whistle! The game is over!) is the commentator's signature moment.

## 9. «Torschützenkönig» — Top scorer

Literally «goal-shooter-king.» Only German could make a compound word this majestic for the league's top scorer. The Torschützenkönig is celebrated like royalty. «Wer wird diesmal Torschützenkönig?» (Who will be top scorer this time?) is debated from August to May.

## 10. «Die Nationalmannschaft» — The national team

«The National Team.» Usually shortened to «Die Nationalelf» (The National Eleven) or simply «Die Mannschaft» (The Team). When Die Nationalmannschaft plays, Germany stops working. Bars fill. Streets empty. Everyone is watching. You are either with Die Mannschaft or you are irrelevant. There is no middle ground.
