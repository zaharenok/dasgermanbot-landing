# German Words Germans Can't Agree On (And the Battles Are Vicious)

You thought learning German was hard? Try being German. There are words in this language that native speakers can't agree on. Not dialects. Not regional differences. Straight-up civil wars over articles, pronunciation, and grammar. Friendships have ended. Families divided. Christmas dinners ruined. Here are the most controversial words in the German language.

## 1. Nutella — der, die, or das?

The most brutal war in German linguistics. Is the chocolate spread masculine, feminine, or neuter? Ferrero themselves refused to pick a side and said "it's a fantasy word." Northern Germans say das Nutella. Southern Germans say die Nutella. Some monsters say der Nutella. There is no right answer and every answer is wrong.

## 2. Keks — singular or plural?

One cookie is ein Keks. Two cookies are zwei Keks. Or is it zwei Kekse? Duden says both are correct. Germans say one is obviously right and the other is obviously wrong — they just disagree on which is which.

## 3. Brötchen — wait, what do you call it?

We covered this. But the actual pronunciation war is real. In Hamburg it's Rundstück. In Bavaria it's Semmel. In Swabia it's Weckle. In Berlin it's Schrippe. Every region has its own word and every region thinks the others are insane.

## 4. Krawatte — how do you pronounce it?

Some Germans say krah-VAH-tuh. Others say KRAH-va-tuh. The stress shifts between first and second syllable depending on where you grew up. Neither side admits the other exists.

## 5. Astronaut — der or die?

A female astronaut. Is it die Astronautin? Or die Astronaut? Can you add -in to a word that already sounds like it ends in -aut? German feminists and grammarians have been arguing about this for decades.

## 6. Dudelsack or Sackpfeife?

Bagpipes. Northern Germans say Dudelsack (doodle bag). Southern Germans say Sackpfeife (sack pipe). Same instrument, two completely different names, and each side thinks their version is the only correct one.

## 7. E-Mail — der or das?

Der E-Mail follows the gender of Mail (from Brief — der Brief). Das E-Mail follows the gender of Post — no wait, that's die Post. Actually, die E-Mail makes sense too because of die Nachricht. Three articles. Zero consensus. Pure chaos.

## 8. Vanille — V or F?

Pronouncing Vanille: do you start with a V sound or an F sound? Van-ill-uh or Fan-ill-uh? Both are technically valid. Germans will judge you based on which you choose and they won't tell you which one they prefer until it's too late.

## 9. Spiegel or Spargel — the vowel trap

No one confuses these words. But southern Germans pronounce the "ie" and "ar" vowels differently from northern Germans. A Bavarian saying Spiegel can sound like they're saying something completely different to a Hamburger.

## 10. Marmelade or Konfitüre?

EU regulation says only citrus-based preserves can be called Marmelade. Strawberry jam must be Konfitüre. Germans collectively ignored this and kept calling everything Marmelade. The EU gave up. German stubbornness: 1, European bureaucracy: 0.
