# German Social Media Vocabulary: 12 Words You Need Online

Textbook German will teach you how to order a Bratwurst and ask for directions to the train station. It will not prepare you for the chaos of a German Instagram comment section. German social media has its own vocabulary — half English loanwords with German grammar glued on, half uniquely German inventions. Here are the words and phrases you need to survive online in German.

## 1. Posten — To Post

The English "to post" got Germanified with a regular -en ending. «Ich poste heute ein neues Foto.» (I'm posting a new photo today.) «Er hat nichts auf Instagram gepostet.» (He didn't post anything on Instagram.) Conjugated exactly like a normal German verb: ich poste, du postest, er postet. Past tense is hat gepostet. Perfectly intuitive.

## 2. Liken — To Like

Same story — English "like" turned into a German verb. «Ich like dein Bild.» (I like your picture.) «Sie hat meinen Kommentar geliked.» (She liked my comment.) Yes, Germans say geliked in the past tense. It looks wrong. It's standard German internet slang now. Even Oma does it.

## 3. Story — Die Story

Germans use "Story" exactly like in English, but with a German article. «Ich gucke gerade deine Story.» (I'm watching your Story right now.) «Meine Story ist in 24 Stunden weg.» (My Story disappears in 24 hours.) Plural is Stories — die Stories. Everyone understands it, and it's used across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook.

## 4. Abonnieren — To Subscribe / Follow

This is the native German word for subscribing or following. «Ich abonniere deinen Kanal.» (I subscribe to your channel.) On Instagram and TikTok, though, "folgen" is more common for "to follow." Abonnieren is the YouTube word. «Abonniert meinen Kanal!» (Subscribe to my channel!) — you'll hear this in every German YouTube video.

## 5. Folgen — To Follow

«Folgen» is how Germans say "to follow" on social media. «Ich folge dir schon seit Jahren.» (I've been following you for years.) «Wie viele Leute folgen dir?» (How many people follow you?) The noun is der Follower, which is a direct loan from English. «Er hat 10.000 Follower.» (He has 10,000 followers.)

## 6. Teilen — To Share

Germans use "teilen" for sharing posts. It's the same word as "to divide" or "to share" in everyday German. «Kannst du den Beitrag teilen?» (Can you share the post?) «Der Post wurde 500 Mal geteilt.» (The post was shared 500 times.) Simple, direct, and common across all platforms.

## 7. Hochladen — To Upload

"Hochladen" literally means "to load up" — the opposite of herunterladen (to download). «Ich habe das Video hochgeladen.» (I uploaded the video.) «Das Hochladen dauert ewig.» (The upload is taking forever.) Germans also use "uploaden" sometimes, but hochladen is the official German term and sounds more natural in most contexts.

## 8. Der Hashtag — The Hashtag

Same word, German pronunciation: "hæʃtæk." «Vergiss nicht den Hashtag zu setzen.» (Don't forget to add the hashtag.) «Welche Hashtags sind gerade trend?» (Which hashtags are trending right now?) Germans love hashtags as much as anyone, and #Montagsmuffel (Monday grouch) is a beloved weekly tradition.

## 9. Streamen — To Stream

Netflix, Spotify, Twitch — Germans streamen everything. «Ich streame gerade die neue Serie.» (I'm streaming the new series right now.) «Streamst du auf Twitch?» (Do you stream on Twitch?) Past tense: hat gestreamt. It's a completely normal German verb now. «Die ganze Familie streamt.» — The whole family streams.

## 10. Der Beitrag / Der Post — The Post

Germans use both der Beitrag (native German) and der Post (English loanword) for a social media post. «Der Beitrag wurde gelöscht.» (The post was deleted.) «Dein Post ist viral gegangen!» (Your post went viral!) Beitrag is more formal, Post is casual. Use Post on Instagram, Beitrag in a business context.

## 11. Verlinken — To Tag / To Link

When you tag someone in a post, you "verlinkst" them. «Ich habe dich im Kommentar verlinkt.» (I tagged you in the comment.) «Kannst du mich verlinken?» (Can you tag me?) The noun is der Link or die Verlinkung. "Markieren" is also used, but verlinken is more common on Facebook, Instagram, and LinkedIn.

## 12. Löschen — To Delete

Löschen is the German word for delete, and it's used everywhere. «Ich habe den Post gelöscht.» (I deleted the post.) «Lösch das besser, bevor es jemand sieht.» (You better delete that before someone sees it.) It can also mean "to extinguish" (like a fire), which makes "Feuer löschen" and "Post löschen" a fun coincidence.
