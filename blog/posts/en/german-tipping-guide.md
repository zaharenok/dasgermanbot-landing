# How to Tip in Germany: The Trinkgeld Rules Nobody Explains

The bill comes. You freeze. Do you tip 20% like in America? 10%? Nothing? The waiter is waiting. Everyone at the table is looking at you. Here's the complete guide to tipping in Germany so you never panic again.

## The basic rule

**5-10%. That's it.** Not 15%, not 20%, not whatever your home country does. In Germany, service is included in the price (bedienung inbegriffen). The tip is a small bonus for good service, not a salary supplement.

**Key difference:** Germans don't leave money on the table. You tell the waiter the total amount you want to pay. They calculate the change from that.

## Restaurants

**Casual restaurant (10-30€ per person):** Round up to the next 5€. Example: bill is 27€, say «30 bitte» or «stimmt so.» That's roughly 10%.

**Nice restaurant (30-60€ per person):** Add 5-10%. Example: bill is 48€, say «50 bitte» or «52.» Not complicated.

**How to actually do it:**
1. Waiter brings the bill
2. You look at the total
3. You say the amount you want to pay: «Das macht 45 Euro, bitte 50.»
4. Waiter brings change if needed
5. Done

**Do NOT** leave cash on the table and walk away. This confuses German waiters. Tell them the amount directly.

## Bars

**Beer or coffee (3-5€):** Round up to the next euro. «Stimmt so» (keep the change) works perfectly.

**Cocktails (8-12€):** Round up by 1-2€. If two cocktails cost 18€, say «20.»

**Pro tip:** At a bar counter, you can leave small change on the counter. The bartender will take it. At a table, tell them.

## Taxis

**Round up to the next 2-5€.** If the fare is 13€, give 15€ and say «stimmt so.» If it's 22€, say «25.» No need to calculate percentages. Just round up a bit.

**Important:** Always tip in cash, even if you pay the fare by card. Tell the driver before they run the card: «Auf 25 bitte.» They'll charge 25€ total.

## Hairdresser

**5-10%**, same as restaurants. If the haircut is 40€, give 42-45€. Say «stimmt so» when handing over the cash.

German hairdressers remember good tippers. You'll get better appointments next time.

## Delivery

**2-3€ for food delivery.** If the pizza is 15€, hand the driver 17-18€ and say «stimmt so.» That's generous and appreciated.

**Package delivery (DHL, Hermes):** No tip expected. If someone carries something heavy up 4 flights of stairs, 1-2€ is nice but not required.

## Hotels

**Housekeeping:** 1-2€ per night, left on the pillow with a note «Danke.»

**Bellboy:** 1-2€ per bag.

**Breakfast buffet:** No tip needed.

## When NOT to tip

- **Supermarket cashiers** — no tip, ever.
- **Bus drivers** — no tip.
- **Fast food (McDonald's, Döner stand)** — no tip.
- **Public bathrooms (50c)** — the fee is the fee. No extra.

## The magic phrases

- **«Stimmt so.»** — Keep the change. (The most useful tipping phrase.)
- **«Das reicht.»** — That's enough. (When they try to give you change back.)
- **«Auf [amount] bitte.»** — Make it [amount] please. (When paying by card.)
- **«Danke, das war sehr lecker.»** — Thank you, that was very delicious. (Say this when tipping at restaurants. Makes the tip feel personal.)

## Card vs cash

Many places in Germany still prefer cash. When paying by card, tell the waiter the total amount BEFORE they enter it into the terminal. Say «Auf 45 bitte» and they'll charge 45€ total (bill + tip in one transaction).

If the terminal is already showing the exact amount, you missed your window. Don't panic. Just say «Danke» and remember for next time.
