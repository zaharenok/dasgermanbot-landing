# German Recycling Rules: How to Sort Your Trash Without Losing Your Mind

You threw a yogurt cup in the wrong bin. Your neighbor noticed. Now there's a passive-aggressive note on your door. Welcome to Mülltrennung — Germany's national obsession with trash sorting. Here's how to survive.

## The 5 bins you need to know

### Gelbe Tonne / Gelber Sack (Yellow bin/bag)

**What goes in:** Plastic packaging, metal cans, aluminum foil, milk cartons, plastic bottles (without Pfand), yogurt cups, foam trays.

**What does NOT go in:** Paper, glass, food waste, electronics, clothes.

**Rule:** Rinse it first. A yogurt cup with yogurt still in it contaminates the whole recycling batch. Quick rinse is enough.

### Blaue Tonne (Blue bin) — Paper

**What goes in:** Newspapers, magazines, cardboard, printer paper, envelopes (even with windows), cereal boxes.

**What does NOT go in:** Used paper towels, tissues, pizza boxes with grease, photos.

### Braune Tonne (Brown bin) — Biomüll / Organic

**What goes in:** Fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds (with filter), tea bags, eggshells, bread, garden waste, flower cuttings.

**What does NOT go in:** Meat, bones, dairy, cooking oil, cat litter.

### Restmüll (Black/gray bin) — General waste

**What goes in:** Everything that doesn't fit the other categories. Diapers, vacuum cleaner bags, broken ceramics, cigarette butts, used tissues.

**What does NOT go in:** Batteries, electronics, paint, chemicals — these go to the Recyclinghof (recycling center).

### Glascontainer (Glass containers) — On the street

Separate containers on street corners, sorted by color: **Weißglas** (white/clear), **Grünglas** (green), **Braunglas** (brown).

**What goes in:** Wine bottles, jam jars, sauce jars, olive oil bottles.

**What does NOT go in:** Ceramics, mirror glass, light bulbs, window glass, Pfand bottles.

**Silence rule:** No glass sorting on Sundays or after 20:00. The crashing noise violates Ruhezeit. Germans take this seriously.

## The Pfandsystem: Getting your money back

This is the genius part. Many bottles and cans have a **Pfand** (deposit):

- **0.25€** — Most plastic bottles, beer cans
- **0.08€ or 0.15€** — Some glass beer bottles
- **No Pfand** — Wine bottles, juice bottles, milk bottles

Look for the **Pfand** logo on the label. If it's there, DON'T throw it in any bin. Take it to a supermarket and feed it into the **Pfandautomat** (reverse vending machine). It prints a receipt. Hand it to the cashier for cash or use it toward your groceries.

**Pro tip:** Crushed bottles may not be accepted by the Pfandautomat. Keep them intact.

## Things you CANNOT throw in any bin

These go to the **Recyclinghof** (recycling center) or **Wertstoffhof**:

- **Batteries** — Also collected in small boxes at supermarket entrances
- **Electronics** — Old phones, laptops, cables
- **Paint and chemicals** — Schadstoffmobil (hazardous waste mobile collection)
- **Old furniture** — Sperrmüll (bulk waste) — schedule a pickup or bring it yourself
- **Clothes** — Altkleidercontainer (clothing donation bins) on street corners

## Sperrmüll: The big stuff

Moving out? Old sofa, mattress, bookshelf? You can't just put it on the street. You need to:

1. Call your local **BSR** (Berlin) or waste management company
2. Schedule a **Sperrmüllabholung** (bulk waste pickup)
3. Put items out only the night before

OR: Put it on the street with a «ZU VERSCHENKEN» (free to take) sign. It'll disappear in 2 hours. Germans love free furniture.

## Fines and neighbor drama

Sorting wrong is not just embarrassing — it can cost you:

- Your trash might not get collected if the wrong items are visible
- Neighbors WILL report you to the landlord or Hausverwaltung
- Repeat offenses can lead to fines
- In extreme cases, the entire building's bin gets flagged and everyone pays more

**The unwritten rule:** If you mess up, your neighbors will know. And they will judge.
