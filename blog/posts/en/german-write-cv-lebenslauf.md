# How to Write a German Lebenslauf (CV): Complete Guide for Expats

You send your American-style CV to a German company. Two weeks of silence. Your CV went straight to the trash because it had no photo, gaps in dates, and was two pages long. German CVs have rules. Strict ones. Here's how to get it right.

## The format: Tabular only

German Lebenslauf means **tabular CV**. Not a paragraph story. Not a creative portfolio. A clean, structured table with dates on the left and content on the right.

**Structure (top to bottom):**

1. Personal information (photo, name, address, contact)
2. Professional experience (Berufserfahrung)
3. Education (Ausbildung / Studium)
4. Skills and qualifications (Kenntnisse)
5. Hobbies (optional — yes, Germans still list these)

## The photo: Required

Yes, in 2026, German CVs still include a professional photo. Top-right corner. Professional headshot, neutral background, friendly but serious expression.

**Where to get one:** Go to a Fotostudio and ask for «Bewerbungsfotos.» Costs 15-30€. Do NOT use a selfie. Germans will judge.

**Anti-discrimination note:** Technically, you can apply without a photo. In practice, many recruiters still expect one.

## Personal info section

Include:
- **Vor- und Nachname** — First and last name
- **Adresse** — Address
- **Telefon** — Phone number
- **E-Mail** — Email
- **Geburtsdatum und -ort** — Date and place of birth
- **Staatsangehörigkeit** — Nationality (optional but helpful for expats)
- **Familienstand** — Marital status (optional)

## Professional experience (Berufserfahrung)

Format: **MM/YYYY – MM/YYYY | Company | Position | Location**

```
08/2020 – 03/2024  Software Developer, Tech GmbH, Berlin
- Developed microservices using Python and Docker
- Led team of 5 developers on customer-facing product
```

**Rules:**
- Reverse chronological order (newest first)
- NO gaps. If you were unemployed, write «Arbeitssuche» (job search) or «Weiterbildung» (further education)
- Bullet points, not paragraphs
- Be specific with numbers and results

## Education (Ausbildung / Studium)

Same format, reverse chronological:

```
10/2016 – 06/2020  Bachelor of Computer Science, TU München
09/2013 – 06/2016  Abitur, Gymnasium Berlin-Mitte
```

Include thesis topic if relevant: **Abschlussarbeit: «Machine Learning in Production Systems»**

## Skills (Kenntnisse)

Group by category:

**Sprachen (Languages):**
- Deutsch: B2 (Goethe-Zertifikat)
- Englisch: Muttersprache
- Russisch: Muttersprache

**EDV / IT-Kenntnisse:**
- Python, JavaScript, Docker, AWS

**Führerschein:** Klasse B (optional but common)

Rate your language levels honestly. B1, B2, C1, C2. German employers WILL test you.

## Things that will get your CV rejected

- **Longer than 2 pages** — One page for under 5 years experience, max two for senior.
- **No photo** — Many will assume you're hiding something.
- **Gaps in dates** — Explain everything. Unemployment, travel, parental leave — list it all.
- **Spelling errors** — Germans are ruthless about this. One typo can cost you.
- **Creative formatting** — No colors, no icons, no infographics. Clean and professional.
- **References to salary** — Never put salary history on a CV.
- **«References available upon request»** — German CVs don't include this phrase. References come separately as Arbeitszeugnisse.

## Hobbies section

Yes, Germans still include this. Keep it short — one line max:

**Hobbys: Joggen, Fotografie, Kochen**

Avoid controversial topics. This section helps recruiters assess cultural fit.

## Save and send as PDF

Always. Never send a Word document. File name: **Lebenslauf_Vorname_Nachname.pdf**
