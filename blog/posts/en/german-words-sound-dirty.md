# 12 German Words That Sound Dirty But Are Completely Innocent

You're sitting in a German cafe. Your friend says „Ich muss mal auf die Toilette, meine Fahrt war lang." Your brain short-circuits. Did they just say what you think they said?

They did not. Welcome to German, where perfectly normal words sound wildly inappropriate to English speakers. Here are 12 of the most confusing ones.

## 1. Die Fahrt (The trip / ride)

Sounds like a bodily function. Means a journey, a ride, a trip. *„Meine Fahrt nach Berlin war lang"* = „My trip to Berlin was long." Every German public transport ticket says *Einzelfahrt* (single ride). Stop laughing.

## 2. Die Hose (Pants / trousers)

Sounds like something you water a garden with. But *die Hose* simply means pants or trousers. *„Ich brauche eine neue Hose"* = „I need new pants." Completely normal. Stop imagining.

## 3. Der Schmuck (Jewelry)

In English, „schmuck" is an insult meaning a foolish person. In German, *der Schmuck* means jewelry or decoration. *„Dein Schmuck ist wunderschön"* = „Your jewelry is beautiful." One of the nicest compliments in German sounds like a terrible insult in English.

## 4. Der Puff (Quilt / duvet)

In English slang, this means something entirely different. In German, *der Puff* is a quilt or duvet. *„Ich brauche einen neuen Puff"* = „I need a new duvet." A perfectly innocent bedtime statement that sounds like a scandal.

## 5. Die Gift (Wait, that's wrong)

Actually it's *das Gift* — and it means poison. The English word „gift" (present) becomes deadly in German. *„Das ist Gift"* = „That's poison." Imagine the confusion at German birthday parties when someone asks for a „Gift."

## 6. Die Domina (Cathedral dome)

Sounds like a dominatrix. But *die Domina* (or more commonly *die Dom*) refers to a cathedral. *„Die Kölner Dom ist wunderschön"* = „The Cologne Cathedral is beautiful." Religious architecture has never sounded so edgy.

## 7. Das Gesicht (Face)

Sounds like „get sh*t" to English ears. But *das Gesicht* means face. *„Du hast ein schönes Gesicht"* = „You have a beautiful face." One of the sweetest things you can say in German sounds like an insult.

## 8. Der Kamm (Comb)

Rhymes with „calm" but sounds like something else entirely to dirty-minded English speakers. *Der Kamm* is simply a comb. *„Ich brauche einen Kamm"* = „I need a comb." Hair care has never been so awkward.

## 9. Werden (To become)

Sounds like a very rude English word starting with V. *Werden* is one of the most important German verbs — it means „to become" and is used to form the future tense. You'll say it dozens of times a day. Try not to giggle.

## 10. Die Mutter (Mother)

Sounds like „mutter" in English — mumbling quietly. In German, *die Mutter* is mother. But here's the real confusion: *die Mutter* is also a nut (the mechanical kind). Context is everything.

## 11. Der Rat (Advice / council)

In English, a rat is a rodent or a snitch. In German, *der Rat* means advice or council. *„Ich brauche deinen Rat"* = „I need your advice." Asking for guidance in German sounds like you're requesting a rodent.

## 12. Das Fat (No, wait)

Actually *fett* with double t — means fat or grease. *„Das Fett in der Pfanne"* = „The fat in the pan." But when a German teenager says *„Krass fett!"* it means „Awesome!" German slang is a beautiful disaster.
