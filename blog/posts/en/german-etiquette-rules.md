# 12 German Etiquette Rules That Will Surprise You

Germans don't just have rules. They have rules about rules. And rules about how to break rules. Some of these will feel strange. Others will make you wish your country had them too. But if you ignore them in Germany, people will notice. Here are the ones that catch foreigners completely off guard.

## 1. Never Wish Happy Birthday Early

Wishing someone happy birthday before the actual day is considered bad luck. Germans take this seriously. Wait until midnight. Or better yet, wait until you see them on the day. Early wishes are not thoughtful — they're a curse.

## 2. Don't Cross on Red — Even at 3 AM

There is no traffic. No cars. Not a single human in sight. The light is red. You wait. Children might be watching. Germans believe that if a child sees an adult cross on red, the child will learn that rules are optional. So they wait. Always.

## 3. Say Your Name When Answering the Phone

"Hello?" is not enough. Germans answer with their last name. "Müller." Or their full name. It saves time. Everyone knows who picked up. No "who is this?" awkwardness. Direct and efficient.

## 4. Bring Your Own Cake to Work on Your Birthday

It's your birthday and you want cake? You bring the cake. To the office. For everyone else. The birthday person provides. Not the other way around. It's not selfish to celebrate yourself — it's expected.

## 5. Separate Your Trash — Or Else

Germans sort trash into categories that would make a philosopher dizzy. Bio, paper, plastic, glass (by color), residual waste. Put something in the wrong bin and a neighbor WILL correct you. Publicly if necessary.

## 6. Don't Start Eating Before Everyone Is Served

At a restaurant or dinner table, you wait. Until every single person has their food in front of them. Then someone says "Guten Appetit" and you begin. Starting early is rude. Not a little rude — noticeably rude.

## 7. Cash Is King

A bakery. A restaurant. A taxi. The bill is 12 euros. You pull out a card. The cashier looks at you like you suggested paying with seashells. Germany runs on cash. Always carry bills.

## 8. Sundays Are Sacred

No shopping. No loud noise. No mowing the lawn. No drilling. Sunday is Ruhetag — rest day. Grocery stores are closed. Pharmacies rotate emergency duty. It's not a suggestion. It's federal law.

## 9. Shake Hands. Always.

Meeting someone? Shake hands. Leaving? Shake hands. Introduced to a group? Shake every hand individually. A wave or nod is not sufficient. Germans find the American hug greeting confusing and slightly alarming.

## 10. Be Punctual — But Not Too Early

15 minutes early is not "polite." It's stressful for your host who isn't ready. 5 minutes early is acceptable. On time is perfect. 5 minutes late requires an apology. 15 minutes late requires a phone call. This is not exaggeration.

## 11. Don't Small Talk at the Checkout

The cashier is scanning your items. You are paying. This is a transaction, not a friendship opportunity. Germans find checkout small talk confusing. "How is your day?" — why are you asking a stranger about their emotional state?

## 12. clink Eyes, Not Just Glasses

When saying "Prost" or "Zum Wohl," you must look the other person directly in the eyes while clinking glasses. No eye contact? Seven years of bad sex. Yes, this is a real superstition that Germans actually believe.
