# 15 Things That Are Rude in Germany (And You Had No Idea)

You think you're being polite. You're not. Germans have unwritten rules about what's rude, and nobody will tell you when you break them. They'll just quietly think you're disrespectful. Here are 15 things you're probably doing wrong.

## 1. Not greeting when you enter a shop

Walk into a small bakery or shop without saying «Guten Tag» and you've already made a bad impression. In Germany, you greet the shopkeeper when you enter. Always.

**Fix:** Say «Guten Tag!» when you walk in. Say «Auf Wiedersehen!» or «Tschüss!» when you leave. Both matter.

## 2. Not saying «Mahlzeit» at lunch

Between 11:30 and 14:00, Germans say **Mahlzeit** to colleagues instead of «Guten Appetit» or «Hallo.» It literally means «meal time» and functions as a lunch greeting. Not saying it back when someone says it to you is rude.

**Fix:** When someone says «Mahlzeit,» you say «Mahlzeit» back. That's it. No translation needed.

## 3. Walking in the bike lane

German sidewalks often have three zones: pedestrian, bike, and sometimes parking. The bike lane is marked with a bicycle symbol and often a different color. Walk in it and a cyclist will ring their bell, yell, or both.

**Fix:** Look down. Stay on the pedestrian side. If you see a bike symbol, move.

## 4. Crossing on red — even when no cars are coming

Jaywalking is a big deal in Germany. Crossing on red, especially with children watching, is considered setting a bad example. Germans will actually say something.

**Fix:** Wait for the green Ampelmännchen. Even at 3am with zero cars.

## 5. Not saying «Guten Appetit» before eating

Before anyone starts eating, someone says **«Guten Appetit!»** and everyone else says it back. Starting to eat before this is rude.

**Fix:** Wait until someone says «Guten Appetit,» say it back, then eat.

## 6. Clinking glasses without eye contact

When saying «Prost!» or «Cheers!», you MUST look the other person in the eyes. Not doing this means 7 years of bad luck — and Germans are only half joking.

**Fix:** Eye contact. Every time. Every person.

## 7. Splitting the bill wrong

Germans split bills precisely. If your meal was 12.50€ and your friend's was 14€, you each pay your exact amount. Suggesting to «just split it 50/50» when your meal was cheaper is considered taking advantage.

**Fix:** Pay for what you ordered. The waiter expects this — they'll ask «Getrennt oder zusammen?»

## 8. Being late without telling anyone

5 minutes late is already late. 10 minutes late without a message is disrespectful. Germans plan their day in blocks, and your lateness shifts everything.

**Fix:** Be on time. If you're running late, message immediately: «Ich komme 10 Minuten später.»

## 9. Making noise on Sunday

Sunday is **Ruhetag** (rest day) in Germany. No lawn mowing, no drilling, no loud music, no washing machine after 22:00. Your neighbors will call the police. Not exaggerating.

**Fix:** Keep Sundays quiet. Do your noisy chores on Saturday.

## 10. Not taking off shoes indoors

Walking into a German home with outdoor shoes is a violation. Even if they say «Oh, you can keep them on» — they're being polite. They want you to take them off.

**Fix:** Always ask: «Soll ich die Schuhe ausziehen?» (Should I take off my shoes?) The answer is always yes.

## 11. Not letting people off the train first

Standing in front of the train door when people are trying to get out. Germans line up to the side and let passengers exit first.

**Fix:** Stand to the side. Let people exit. Then board.

## 12. Small talk at the checkout

The cashier doesn't want to hear about your day. German cashiers are fast and efficient. Chatting holds up the line and annoys everyone behind you.

**Fix:** Greet, pay, say thank you, leave. «Danke, schönen Tag noch!» is the maximum.

## 13. Using «Du» with strangers

**Du** is for friends, family, children, and fellow students. Everyone else is **Sie.** Using «Du» with a stranger, especially someone older, is disrespectful.

**Fix:** When in doubt, use «Sie.» They'll offer «Wir können uns duzen» if they want to switch.

## 14. Not recycling properly

Germany has the most complex recycling system in the world. Throwing plastic in the general trash, or not rinsing a yogurt cup before recycling it, is practically a sin.

**Fix:** Learn the bins: Gelber Sack (plastic/metal), Papier (paper), Biomüll (organic), Restmüll (general), Glas (glass at public containers).

## 15. Complimenting and not meaning it

Germans take compliments literally. If you say «We should definitely hang out sometime!» — they'll expect you to follow up with a date and time. Empty politeness is worse than no compliment.

**Fix:** Only offer what you mean. A genuine «Ich freue mich, dich kennengelernt zu haben» is worth more than 10 fake «let's grab coffee sometime.»
