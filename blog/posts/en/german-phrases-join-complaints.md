# German Phrases You Need When Germans Start Complaining

Germans have many hobbies: hiking, bread, bureaucracy. But their true national sport? Complaining. The weather is wrong. The train is late. The neighbor's lawn is two centimeters too tall. Complaining is how Germans bond. If you want to be accepted, you need to learn how to complain like one. Here are the phrases that will make Germans think you've lived here forever.

## 1. "Das Wetter ist scheiße."

The foundation of all German small talk. The weather is always wrong. Too hot, too cold, too wet, too dry. It doesn't matter what the actual weather is — say this and every German within earshot will nod in solidarity. Bonus points if you add «schon wieder» (again). «Das Wetter ist schon wieder scheiße.» Now you're practically a local.

## 2. "Die Bahn ist schon wieder zu spät."

The second pillar of German complaint culture. Deutsche Bahn being late is not an inconvenience — it's a lifestyle. Say this at any gathering and watch Germans compete over who has the worst train story. «Ich hab letzte Woche drei Stunden am Gleis gestanden.» Instant connection.

## 3. "Alles wird immer teurer."

Everything is getting more expensive. Rent, groceries, beer. This phrase works in every era, every city, every conversation. A German grandmother said this. Her grandmother said this. It's eternally true and eternally complainable. Use it and watch the heads nod.

## 4. "Die Jugend von heute..."

The youth of today. Fill in whatever complaint you want. Don't know how to use a broom. Always on their phones. No respect. This phrase is a rite of passage. When you say it unironically, you've officially integrated into German society. Congratulations.

## 5. "Früher war alles besser."

Everything used to be better. The ultimate German complaint. No specifics needed. No era specified. Just a general nostalgia for a time that probably wasn't actually better but feels better in retrospect. Say this with a sigh and a head shake. You're in.

## 6. "Man kann sich hier gar nicht mehr bewegen vor Touristen."

You can't move here anymore because of all the tourists. Say this in any popular city — Berlin, Munich, Hamburg. The irony of being a foreigner complaining about foreigners is completely lost on Germans. They will agree enthusiastically.

## 7. "Der Nachbar hat schon wieder den Rasen gemäht. Am Sonntag!"

The neighbor mowed the lawn. On a Sunday. This is the gravest sin in German culture. Sunday is Ruhetag — rest day. No noise. No work. No lawn mowing. If you complain about Sunday noise violations, Germans will adopt you immediately.

## 8. "Die Politik ist ein einziger Scherbenhaufen."

Politics is a single pile of shards. Germans love complaining about politics but hate discussing actual solutions. Just shake your head, say this phrase, and take another sip of beer. You've mastered the art.

## 9. "Ich hab keinen Bock mehr."

I'm so done. I've had enough. The universal German exhaustion phrase. Use it after work, after shopping, after reading the news. «Kein Bock» is the emotion that unites all Germans regardless of age, region, or political affiliation.

## 10. "Sag mal, geht's noch?"

Excuse me, is this for real? The nuclear option of German complaints. Use when someone cuts in line, drives too fast, or does anything unacceptable. «Geht's noch?» is pure disbelief wrapped in aggression. Say it loud enough and other Germans will join your cause.
