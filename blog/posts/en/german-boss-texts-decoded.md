# German Texts From Your Boss: What They Say vs What They Mean

Working in Germany means one thing: your boss will send you messages that sound polite but mean something completely different. German corporate communication is an art form built on indirect criticism wrapped in formal grammar. You need a decoder ring. Here are the messages you'll receive and what they actually mean.

## 1. "Wir müssen reden."

What it says: We need to talk.
What it means: You messed up and this is a warning. Nobody says «wir müssen reden» to give you a compliment. Start preparing your defense immediately.

## 2. "Das haben wir schon immer so gemacht."

What it says: We've always done it this way.
What it means: Your idea is rejected before you finished explaining it. This is the ultimate conversation ender in German offices. Innovation stops here. Tradition wins.

## 3. "Ich dachte, das wäre deine Aufgabe."

What it says: I thought that was your task.
What it means: You forgot something important and I'm not going to say directly that you failed. Instead I'll frame it as my confusion so you feel worse. German guilt delivery at its finest.

## 4. "Können wir das kurz besprechen?"

What it says: Can we discuss this briefly?
What it means: This will not be brief. This will be a 45-minute meeting where your work gets dissected. «Kurz» in German corporate language means «until I'm satisfied,» which is never brief.

## 5. "Das ist eine gute Idee, aber..."

What it says: That's a good idea, but...
What it means: Your idea is terrible. The «aber» erases everything before it. In German business communication, anything before «aber» is decorative. Only what comes after matters.

## 6. "Da müssen wir nochmal drüber schauen."

What it says: We need to look at that again.
What it means: Your work was rejected. Not «needs revision.» Rejected. But saying it directly would be rude, so instead it becomes «let's look again.» You'll be looking again indefinitely.

## 7. "Ich bin erreichbar."

What it says: I'm available.
What it means: I'm watching. I see your messages. Don't pretend you didn't get my email. This is a threat disguised as helpfulness. Your boss is online and expects a response within minutes.

## 8. "Ist das dein Ernst?"

What it says: Are you serious?
What it means: What you just proposed is so far from acceptable that your boss needs confirmation you're not joking. You should probably pretend you were indeed joking and start over.

## 9. "Lass uns das auf nächste Woche verschieben."

What it says: Let's push that to next week.
What it means: This is not important enough for me to deal with now and probably never will be. «Next week» in German corporate means «I hope you forget about this.»

## 10. "Das ist nicht mein Bereich."

What it says: That's not my area.
What it means: I'm not helping you. This is your problem now. German corporate boundaries are iron-clad. Your boss just built a wall between your question and their responsibility.
