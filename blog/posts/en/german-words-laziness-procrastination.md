# German Words For Laziness And Procrastination That Are Too Relatable

Germany is famous for efficiency. Punctuality. Productivity. This is a lie. Well, half a lie. Germans are efficient at work. But at home? On weekends? During holidays? Germans have elevated doing nothing into an art form. And they have the vocabulary to prove it. Here are German words for laziness and procrastination that prove Germans understand you better than you think.

## 1. «Faulenzer»

The classic. «Faulenzer» means lazy person. From «faul» (lazy, rotten) and the suffix that makes it a person. A Faulenzer doesn't just avoid work. They have made avoidance their identity. You don't call someone a Faulenzer as an insult. You call them that because it's the truth, and everyone accepts it. «Er ist ein Faulenzer» is not a judgment. It's a diagnosis.

## 2. «Faulpelz»

Literally: lazy fur. A softer, more affectionate version of Faulenzer. «Pelz» means fur, like an animal's coat. A Faulpelz is someone who lies around like a lazy cat, warm and content, refusing to move. Parents use it about children who won't get out of bed. Partners use it about each other on Sunday mornings. It's adorable. It's accurate. It's you, every weekend.

## 3. «Schlafmütze»

Literally: sleepy cap. A sleepyhead. «Mütze» is a cap or hat. «Schlaf» is sleep. The image: someone wearing a sleeping cap, eyes half-closed, brain not yet operational. Used for anyone who's slow to wake up, slow to function, or just generally not firing on all cylinders. Every office has a Schlafmütze who needs three coffees before becoming a person.

## 4. «Trödeln»

To dawdle. To waste time. To take forever doing something that should take five minutes. Children trödeln when getting ready for school. Partners trödeln when it's time to leave. You trödeln when you should be working but instead rearrange your desk. «Hör auf zu trödeln!» (Stop dawdling!) is heard in every German household at least once daily.

## 5. «Müßiggang»

Idleness. Doing absolutely nothing. Not resting, not relaxing — just existing without purpose. «Müßiggang» has a philosophical quality. There's a German saying: «Müßiggang ist aller Laster Anfang» — Idleness is the beginning of all vice. Germans know this proverb. They quote it. And then they continue doing nothing because Müßiggang feels too good.

## 6. «Nichtstun»

Literally: nothing-doing. The German word for doing nothing. Not in a meditative, intentional way. In a «I have things to do but I'm choosing not to do them» way. «Ich bin am Nichtstun» is a legitimate weekend activity in Germany. It goes on the calendar. Right after brunch. Right before the afternoon nap.

## 7. «Rumliegen»

To lie around. «Rum» means around. «Liegen» means to lie. Together: lying around, taking up space, horizontal and proud. A weekend activity. A holiday activity. A Tuesday evening activity when the motivation has left the building. «Ich liege nur rum» (I'm just lying around) is the most honest sentence in the German language.

## 8. «Bummeln»

To stroll, to dawdle, to take forever. «Bummeln» is walking slowly with zero urgency. A «Bummel» through the city is not a walk. It's a resistance against the concept of speed. Shopping becomes «bummeln» when you visit every store and buy nothing. Sunday walks become «bummeln» when you stop at every bench.

## 9. «Verschleppen»

To let things drag on. To postpone so long that the task becomes an emergency. «Ich habe das verschleppt» means «I put this off until it became a crisis.» Every German student has verschleppt an assignment. Every German adult has verschleppt a doctor's appointment. It's universal. The word just gives it a name.

## 10. «Sonntagsruhe»

Sunday rest. Not a suggestion. A legally protected institution. On Sundays in Germany, shops are closed. Loud activities are forbidden. The entire country collectively agrees to do nothing. «Sonntagsruhe» is not laziness. It's the law. Germany literally legislated chill. You cannot vacuum. You cannot drill. You cannot recycle glass bottles because they make noise. You must rest. This is peak German efficiency: making relaxation mandatory.
