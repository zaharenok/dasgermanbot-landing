# What German Dogs Say (And Other Animal Sounds You've Been Getting Wrong)

Every language has animal sounds. But German animal sounds are on another level. They're not just different from English — they reveal how Germans hear the world. A German dog doesn't go "woof woof." A German frog doesn't go "ribbit." And a German rooster? Completely different bird. Here are the animal sounds that will make you question everything.

## 1. Dog: Wau Wau

Not "woof woof." Not "bow wow." Wau wau. Rhymes with "cow cow." Simple, direct, efficient — very German. A small dog is still wau wau. A big dog is still wau wau. German dogs don't discriminate by size.

## 2. Cat: Miau

Close to English "meow" but pronounced MEE-ow with a longer first syllable. German cats are elegant even in their sounds. A Persian cat and a street cat both say miau. Equality in cat sounds.

## 3. Cow: Muh

English: "moo." German: "muh." One syllable. Short. Final. A German cow has no time for extra vowels. Muh. Done. Next.

## 4. Pig: Oink Grunz

English pigs oink. German pigs grunzen — they grunt. The sound is "grunz grunz." It sounds like a tiny engine struggling to start. Very appropriate for pigs.

## 5. Frog: Quak

Not "ribbit." Not "croak." Quak. Like a duck having an identity crisis. German frogs quak and they're confident about it. No other frog sound is acceptable.

## 6. Duck: Ente

Wait, the animal is called Ente. But the sound? Quak quak — same as the frog. In German, ducks and frogs make the same noise. This has never bothered a single German person.

## 7. Rooster: Kikeriki

Not "cock-a-doodle-doo." Kikeriki. Four syllables of pure German morning energy. It sounds like a cheer at a football match. A German rooster doesn't announce the morning — he celebrates it.

## 8. Horse: Wiehern

English: "neigh." German: wiehern. The verb means "to whinny." The actual sound Germans write is "wieh wieh." It sounds like someone trying to spell "we" while sneezing.

## 9. Bird: Tweep Tweep or Piep Piep

Small birds go piep piep. It's adorable. It sounds exactly like what a tiny bird should say. Bigger birds go tweet tweet. Eagles presumably just stare at you in silence.

## 10. Bee: Summ Summ

English bees buzz. German bees summsen. The sound is "summ summ." It sounds like a bee doing mathematics. Very German — even the insects are calculating.

## 11. Owl: Uhu

The owl says uhu. This is also the German word for the eagle owl species. So the owl is essentially just introducing itself. "Who are you?" "Uhu." "Nice to meet you, Uhu."

## 12. Mouse: Piep

Mice go piep. Same as small birds. In German, mice and sparrows sound identical. A mouse in your kitchen and a bird at your window make the same complaint about your cooking.
