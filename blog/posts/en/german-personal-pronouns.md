# German Personal Pronouns: Complete Guide to Personalpronomen for Beginners

You're learning German. You want to talk about yourself and others.

You freeze. **Ich bin... Du bist... Er ist...** How do you use "I", "you", "he", "she", "we", "they" correctly in German?

German personal pronouns (Personalpronomen) are essential for communication — from introducing yourself to talking about others.

**The solution:** Master German personal pronouns with clear explanations, proper cases, and cultural context.

## First Person Pronouns

**Talking about yourself:**

1. **Ich** (I)
   - **Nominative:** "Ich bin Student." (I am a student.)
   - **Accusative:** "Mich siehst du." (You see me.)
   - **Dative:** "Du gibst mir das Buch." (You give me the book.)
   - **Genitive:** "Das ist meines Vaters Auto." (That is my father's car - implied)

2. **Wir** (we)
   - **Nominative:** "Wir gehen ins Kino." (We go to the cinema.)
   - **Accusative:** "Besuch uns bitte." (Please visit us.)
   - **Dative:** "Helft uns bitte." (Please help us.)
   - **Genitive:** "Unser Haus ist groß." (Our house is big.)

## Second Person Pronouns

**Addressing others:**

### Informal (Du)

3. **Du** (you - singular informal)
   - **Use:** Friends, family, children, people you know well
   - **Nominative:** "Du bist freundlich." (You are friendly.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe dich." (I see you.)
   - **Dative:** "Ich kaufe dir ein Geschenk." (I buy you a gift.)
   - **Genitive:** "Dein Auto ist rot." (Your car is red.)

4. **Ihr** (you - plural informal)
   - **Use:** Addressing multiple people informally
   - **Nominative:** "Ihr seid nett." (You are nice.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe euch." (I see you [plural].)
   - **Dative:** "Ich helfe euch." (I help you [plural].)
   - **Genitive:** "Eure Hunde sind süß." (Your dogs are cute.)

### Formal (Sie)

5. **Sie** (you - formal)
   - **Use:** Strangers, business settings, older people, polite address
   - **Nominative:** "Sind Sie Herr Müller?" (Are you Mr. Müller?)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe Sie." (I see you [formal].)
   - **Dative:** "Ich helfe Ihnen." (I help you [formal].)
   - **Genitive:** "Ihre Kinder sind lieb." (Your children are sweet.)
   - **Note:** Always capitalized, even in the middle of a sentence!

## Third Person Pronouns

**Talking about others:**

### Masculine

6. **Er** (he)
   - **Use:** Male person, masculine noun
   - **Nominative:** "Er ist mein Bruder." (He is my brother.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe ihn." (I see him.)
   - **Dative:** "Ich helfe ihm." (I help him.)
   - **Genitive:** "Sein Name ist Thomas." (His name is Thomas.)

### Feminine

7. **Sie** (she)
   - **Use:** Female person, feminine noun
   - **Nominative:** "Sie ist meine Schwester." (She is my sister.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe sie." (I see her.)
   - **Dative:** "Ich helfe ihr." (I help her.)
   - **Genitive:** "Ihr Name ist Anna." (Her name is Anna.)
   - **Note:** Context distinguishes from formal "Sie"

### Neuter

8. **Es** (it)
   - **Use:** Neuter noun, impersonal subject
   - **Nominative:** "Das Buch ist interessant." (The book is interesting.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich lese es." (I read it.)
   - **Dative:** "Es gehört mir." (It belongs to me.)
   - **Genitive:** "Sein Umschlag ist braun." (Its cover is brown.)

### Plural

9. **Sie** (they)
   - **Use:** Multiple people or mixed group
   - **Nominative:** "Sie kommen morgen." (They come tomorrow.)
   - **Accusative:** "Ich sehe sie." (I see them.)
   - **Dative:** "Ich helfe ihnen." (I help them.)
   - **Genitive:** "Ihr Haus ist groß." (Their house is big.)
   - **Note:** Same form as formal "you" - context distinguishes

## Possessive Pronouns

**Showing ownership:**

10. **Mein** (my) - masculine
    - **Example:** "Mein Auto" (my car)

11. **Meine** (my) - feminine
    - **Example:** "Meine Mutter" (my mother)

12. **Mein** (my) - neuter
    - **Example:** "Mein Buch" (my book)

13. **Meine** (my) - plural
    - **Example:** "Meine Freunde" (my friends)

14. **Dein** (your) - informal singular
    - **Example:** "Dein Name" (your name - informal)

15. **Eure** (your) - informal plural
    - **Example:** "Eure Eltern" (your parents - informal)

16. **Ihr** (your) - formal
    - **Example:** "Ihre Name" (your name - formal)
    - **Note:** Always capitalized for formal "your"

17. **Sein** (his/its) - masculine/neuter
    - **Example:** "Sein Hund" (his dog)

18. **Ihr** (her) - feminine
    - **Example:** "Ihr Tasche" (her purse)

19. **Ihr** (their) - plural
    - **Example:** "Ihr Haus" (their house)

20. **Unser** (our)
    - **Example:** "Unser Garten" (our garden)

## Reflexive Pronouns

**Referring back to subject:**

21. **Mich** (myself) - accusative
    - **Example:** "Ich sehe mich im Spiegel." (I see myself in the mirror.)

22. **Dir** (yourself) - dative
    - **Example:** "Kauf dir etwas Schönes!" (Buy yourself something nice!)

23. **Sich** (himself/herself/itself/themselves)
    - **Example:** "Er rasiert sich." (He shaves himself.)
    - **Example:** "Wir waschen uns." (We wash ourselves.)

## Formal vs. Informal Address

**When to use which:**

### Use Du/Ihr (informal):
- Family members
- Close friends
- Children and teenagers
- Fellow students
- People your age or younger in casual settings
- Colleagues in informal work environments

### Use Sie (formal):
- Strangers
- Business contacts
- Older people
- Authority figures
- In shops, restaurants, official settings
- First meeting someone

## Cultural Notes

### German Pronoun Culture

- **Sie is always capitalized:** Unlike other pronouns, formal Sie is always capitalized
- **Du can be offensive:** Using du with strangers may seem rude or overly familiar
- **Offering du:** Older people may suggest, "Wir können uns duzen" (We can say du to each other)
- **Regional differences:** Some regions (like Berlin) are more du-friendly

### Age and Du vs. Sie

- **Children:** Always du until adulthood
- **Teens:** du with peers, Sie with adults
- **Adults:** Sie until offered du
- **Coworkers:** Varies by company culture
- **Online:** Germans often use du on social media

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Wrong Case

❌ "Ich sehe du" (incorrect)
✅ "Ich sehe dich" (correct)

**Rule:** Accusative "dich" after seeing "sehe".

### Mistake 2: Confusing sie/Sie

❌ Using wrong sie/Sie in writing
✅ Capitalized Sie = formal you, lowercase sie = she/they

**Rule:** Context and capitalization show the difference.

### Mistake 3: Wrong Reflexive

❌ "Ich wasche mich" (incorrect)
✅ "Ich wasche mich" (correct)

**Rule:** Use mich for reflexive with ich.

## The Bottom Line

German personal pronouns follow clear patterns:

1. **First person** — Ich, Wir (talking about self/group)
2. **Second person informal** — Du, Ihr (friends, family)
3. **Second person formal** — Sie (polite, strangers)
4. **Third person** — Er, Sie, Es (he, she, it)
5. **Plural** — Sie (they)
6. **Cases** — Nominative, Accusative, Dative, Genitive
7. **Possessives** — Mein, Dein, Sein, Ihr, Unser, Euer, Ihr

**Pro tip:** Start with **Ich, Du, Sie, Er, Sie, Es, Wir** — these seven pronouns cover most basic conversations. And remember: German pronouns change based on grammatical case, so always learn them with their case endings!
