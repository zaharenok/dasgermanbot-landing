# German Words That Sound Wrong in English (But Are Totally Innocent)

You're in class. The teacher says «Fahrt». The entire room giggles. Except the German teacher. Because Fahrt just means «journey». Welcome to German, where half the vocabulary sounds like you shouldn't say it in public. Here are the words that confuse English speakers the most — and what they actually mean.

## The classics

**die Fahrt** — journey, trip, ride
- "Gute Fahrt!" — Have a good trip! (Yes, Germans say this. With a straight face.)
- "Wie lange ist die Fahrt?" — How long is the ride?

**der Schmuck** — jewelry
- "Dein Schmuck ist schön." — Your jewelry is beautiful.
- Not what you think. It's the standard word for jewelry. Say it proudly.

**das Gift** — poison
- "Das ist Gift." — That's poison.
- In English it means a present. In German it'll kill you. Don't mix these up.

**dick** — thick, fat
- "Das Buch ist sehr dick." — The book is very thick.
- Just means «thick». Describing a book, a jacket, a slice of cake. Completely normal.

## Body and clothing

**die Hose** — pants, trousers
- "Ich brauche eine neue Hose." — I need new pants.
- Sounds like «hose». It means pants. Every pair of pants is a Hose.

**der Slip** — underwear (men's briefs)
- Standard word for briefs. Nothing weird about it in German.

**der Po** — butt
- "Setz dich auf den Po." — Sit on your butt.
- The normal, child-friendly word for buttocks. Germans use it casually.

## At the restaurant

**die Torte** — cake (large, layered)
- "Möchtest du ein Stück Torte?" — Would you like a piece of cake?
- Has nothing to do with torture. It's cake. Delicious cake.

**die Nüsse** — nuts
- "Ich bin allergisch gegen Nüsse." — I'm allergic to nuts.
- Just nuts. The food. Move along.

**das Menü** — set menu, combo meal
- "Ich nehme das Menü." — I'll take the set menu.
- Not a «menu» in English sense. The menu is «die Speisekarte». Menü is a multi-course deal.

## At work and school

**die Praxis** — doctor's office, practice
- "Die Praxis öffnet um 8 Uhr." — The practice opens at 8.
- Sounds philosophical. It's just a doctor's office.

**die Conseille** — personal consultation
- Not what it sounds like. It means a private advisory session.

**der Rat** — advice, council
- "Ich brauche deinen Rat." — I need your advice.
- Not a rat. Ratte is rat. Rat is advice. Very different.

## The one that catches everyone

**ich bin heiß** — literally «I am hot» but in German this means «I am horny». To say you're warm, say **mir ist heiß** (to me is hot). This mistake is so common it's practically a rite of passage.

Similar trap:
- **ich bin kalt** = I'm a cold person. Say **mir ist kalt** = I feel cold.
- **ich bin gut** = I'm good (in bed). Say **mir geht es gut** = I'm doing well.

## Why this matters

These false-friend moments aren't just funny — they're the exact moments where you either freeze or laugh and move on. The Germans around you have heard these mistakes before. They won't judge. Probably.
