# German Grammar Rules That Even Germans Break

You stress about getting every case right. Meanwhile, your German colleague just wrote «wegen dem» in a work email. Turns out, Germans break their own grammar rules all the time. Here are the ones that prove you can relax a little.

## 1. Wegen + Dativ instead of Genitiv

**The rule:** «wegen des Wetters» (because of the weather) — genitive.

**What Germans actually say:** «wegen dem Wetter» — dative. Everyone does it. In spoken German, the genitive is slowly dying. Your teacher will mark it wrong on a test. Your German friends will say it exactly like that five minutes later.

Even the Duden (the official German dictionary) now accepts both. If the Duden gives up, so can you.

## 2. Das vs Dass — the eternal typo

**das** — the (article): «das Haus», «das ist schön»
**dass** — that (conjunction): «ich weiß, dass du kommst»

Germans mix these up constantly. In WhatsApp messages, emails, even printed signs. It's the German equivalent of your/you're. If a native speaker corrects your dass/das, check their Instagram captions first.

## 3. Seid vs Seit

**seit** — since: «seit drei Jahren»
**seid** — you (plural) are: «ihr seid müde»

This one triggers Germans online. There are entire meme accounts dedicated to spotting «seid» when it should be «seit». Yet every day, thousands of native speakers get it wrong.

## 4. Den vs Denen vs Dem

Relative clauses in German are brutal. Even natives hesitate:

- «Der Mann, **der** da steht» — The man who stands there
- «Der Mann, **den** ich meine» — The man who I mean
- «Die Leute, **denen** ich vertraue» — The people who I trust

In casual speech, Germans often just say «die» for everything: «Die Leute, die ich meine». Not correct. Extremely common.

## 5. Als vs Wie — the comparison trap

**The rule:** «als» for different things («größer als»), «wie» for similar things («so groß wie»).

**Reality:** Germans mess this up constantly. You'll hear «größer wie» everywhere. It's technically wrong. It's also technically everywhere.

## 6. Genitiv disappearing from daily speech

The genitive case is being replaced by «von + Dativ» everywhere:

- Instead of «die Freundin **meines Bruders**» — my brother's girlfriend
- Germans say «die Freundin **von meinem Bruder**»

Formal writing still requires genitive. Casual conversation has mostly moved on.

## 7. Ending phrases with prepositions

**The rule:** Don't end sentences with prepositions.

**Reality:** «Wo kommst du her?» «Was machst du mit?» Germans end sentences with prepositions constantly. It sounds natural. The «correct» version often sounds stiff.

## What this means for you

If native speakers break these rules daily, your mistakes are not a disaster. Germans will never judge a learner for mixing up cases when they themselves can't keep «seid» and «seit» straight. Learn the rules. Then learn when to break them. That's real fluency.
