# Autobahn Survival Guide: German Driving Phrases, Road Signs and Unwritten Rules

The myth: no speed limit on the Autobahn. The truth: about 30% has no limit. The rest will cost you. Germany takes driving seriously — cameras everywhere, strict rules, and a vocabulary you need to know before you get behind the wheel.

## Renting a car

- **Ich möchte ein Auto mieten.** — I'd like to rent a car.
- **Für wie viele Tage?** — For how many days?
- **Mit Vollkaskoversicherung, bitte.** — With comprehensive insurance, please.
- **Haben Sie ein Navigationsgerät?** — Do you have a GPS?
- **Ist die Kaution inklusive?** — Is the deposit included?

You'll need your license and a credit card. Non-EU licenses work for 6 months in Germany. After that, you may need a German one.

## Autobahn basics

The Autobahn is not a free-for-all. Key rules:

- **Right lane is for driving. Left lane is for passing.** Not a suggestion — it's the law. Staying in the left lane without passing is **Mittelspurenfahren** and will earn you dirty looks at minimum, a fine at worst.
- **No speed limit zones** start with a white sign showing four lanes. When you see a round sign with a number — that's your limit.
- **Richtgeschwindigkeit 130** — Recommended speed 130 km/h. Not mandatory, but if you crash going faster, it's partly your fault legally.

Key vocabulary:

- **Ausfahrt** — Exit
- **Einfahrt** — Entrance
- **Baustelle** — Construction zone (everywhere, always)
- **Stau** — Traffic jam
- **Umleitung** — Detour
- **Raststätte** — Rest stop

## Road signs you'll see

German road signs use shapes, not words, for most warnings. But some have text:

- **Einbahnstraße** — One-way street
- **Sackgasse** — Dead end
- **Umleitungen** — Detours
- **Tempolimit** — Speed limit
- **Überholverbot** — No passing
- **Einfahrt freihalten** — Keep entrance clear
- **Anlieger frei** — Residents only (but you can enter to park/load)

## Traffic stops and police

If police pull you over:

- **Ihre Papiere, bitte.** — Your documents, please.
- **Haben Sie getrunken?** — Have you been drinking?
- **Ich habe nichts getrunken.** — I haven't been drinking.
- **Darf ich den Führerschein sehen?** — May I see your license?
- **Das war ein Verkehrsverstoß.** — That was a traffic violation.

Germany has mobile speed cameras (**Blitzer**) everywhere. You won't see them. You'll get a letter weeks later. The word for the fine: **Bußgeld**.

## Breakdown phrases

- **Mein Auto ist kaputt.** — My car is broken down.
- **Ich habe eine Panne.** — I have a breakdown.
- **Können Sie einen Abschleppwagen schicken?** — Can you send a tow truck?
- **Wo ist die nächste Tankstelle?** — Where is the nearest gas station?
- **Ich brauche Benzin.** — I need gas.

On the Autobahn, use the orange emergency phones (**Notrufsäule**) every 2 km. They connect you directly to highway patrol.

## Parking

Parking in German cities is its own nightmare.

- **Parkplatz** — Parking lot
- **Parkhaus** — Parking garage
- ** Parkscheibe** — Parking disc (blue, mandatory in some zones — set it to your arrival time)
- **Parkticket** — Parking ticket
- **Anwohnerparken** — Resident parking only
- **Parkverbot** — No parking

The **Parkscheibe** catches every tourist. Buy one at a gas station (€2). Without it, you'll get a fine even in free parking zones.
