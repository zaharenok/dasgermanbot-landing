# German Adjective Endings: Complete Guide to Strong, Weak, and Mixed Declension

Adjective endings are one of the most challenging aspects of German grammar. There are three different types of declension, and knowing which one to use can be confusing at first.

**The good news:** German adjective endings follow logical patterns. Once you understand when to use strong, weak, or mixed declension, you'll be able to apply the correct endings automatically.

## Why German Adjective Endings Are Tricky

In English, adjectives never change:
- "the **good** car"
- "a **good** car"
- "good cars"

In German, adjectives change based on:
- The article used (definite, indefinite, or no article)
- The case (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive)
- The gender (masculine, feminine, neuter, plural)

**Example:**
- der gut**e** Wagen (the good car)
- ein gut**er** Wagen (a good car)
- gut**er** Wagen (good car)

## The Three Types of Adjective Declension

### 1. Strong Declension (Starke Deklination)

Used when there's **no article** before the adjective.

**When to use:**
- No article at all
- With indefinite words that don't show gender/case (kein, mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr — BUT only when these are used as pronouns, not articles)

**Strong endings:** 
- Nominative: -er, -e, -es, -e (m, f, n, pl)
- Accusative: -en, -e, -es, -e
- Dative: -em, -er, -em, -en
- Genitive: -en, -er, -en, -er

### 2. Weak Declension (Schwache Deklination)

Used when there's a **definite article** (der, die, das, or den) or similar words (dieser, jener, jeder, mancher, welcher, alle).

**When to use:**
- After der/die/das (definite articles)
- After dieser, jener, jeder, mancher, welcher, alle

**Weak endings:**
- Nominative: -e, -e, -e, -en (m, f, n, pl)
- Accusative: -en, -e, -e, -en
- Dative: -en, -en, -en, -en
- Genitive: -en, -en, -en, -en

**Key pattern:** Almost all weak endings are -en!

### 3. Mixed Declension (Gemischte Deklination)

Used with **indefinite articles** (ein, eine, ein) or possessive adjectives (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr).

**When to use:**
- After ein/eine (indefinite articles)
- After possessive adjectives (mein, dein, etc.)

**Mixed endings:**
- Nominative: -er, -e, -es, -en (m, f, n, pl)
- Accusative: -en, -e, -es, -en
- Dative: -en, -en, -en, -en
- Genitive: -en, -en, -en, -en

## When to Use Which Declension

**The Golden Rule:**

Look at what comes BEFORE the adjective:

| Before Adjective | Declension | Example |
|------------------|------------|---------|
| Nothing | Strong | gut**er** Wein |
| der/die/das (definite) | Weak | der gut**e** Wein |
| ein/kein/mein (indefinite) | Mixed | ein gut**er** Wein |
| Possessive (mein, dein, etc.) | Mixed | mein gut**er** Wein |

## Quick Decision Flow

1. **Is there an article?**
   - No → Strong declension
   - Yes → Continue to step 2

2. **What type of article?**
   - Definite (der/die/das) → Weak declension
   - Indefinite (ein/kein/mein) → Mixed declension

## Adjective Endings by Case

### Nominative (Subject)

| Type | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Strong** | -er | -e | -es | -e |
| **Weak** | -e | -e | -e | -en |
| **Mixed** | -er | -e | -es | -en |

**Examples:**
- Das ist ein gut**er** Wein (mixed)
- Das ist der gut**e** Wein (weak)
- Das ist gut**er** Wein (strong)

### Accusative (Direct Object)

| Type | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Strong** | -en | -e | -es | -e |
| **Weak** | -en | -e | -e | -en |
| **Mixed** | -en | -e | -es | -en |

**Examples:**
- Ich sehe ein**en** gut**en** Wein (mixed)
- Ich sehe den gut**en** Wein (weak)
- Ich sehe gut**en** Wein (strong)

### Dative (Indirect Object)

| Type | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Strong** | -em | -er | -em | -en |
| **Weak** | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| **Mixed** | -en | -en | -en | -en |

**Examples:**
- Mit ein**em** gut**en** Wein (mixed)
- Mit dem gut**en** Wein (weak)
- Mit gut**em** Wein (strong)

**Key:** All dative adjectives end in -en or -em!

### Genitive (Possession)

| Type | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Strong** | -en | -er | -en | -er |
| **Weak** | -en | -en | -en | -en |
| **Mixed** | -en | -en | -en | -en |

**Examples:**
- Die Farbe ein**es** gut**en** Weines (mixed)
- Die Farbe des gut**en** Weines (weak)
- Die Farbe gut**en** Weines (strong)

## Special Cases

### After "kein" (No/Not a)

"Kein" behaves like "ein" (indefinite article):

- Ich habe kein**en** gut**en** Wein (mixed declension)
- Ich habe kein gut**en** Wein (strong declension — kein can be used as pronoun)

### After Possessive Adjectives

Mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr, Ihr behave like "ein":

- Mein gut**er** Freund (mixed)
- Deine gut**e** Freundin (mixed)
- Sein gut**es** Auto (mixed)

### Predicate Adjectives (After verbs)

When adjectives come after verbs (sein, werden, bleiben), they take **no ending**:

- Der Wein ist gut (not "gute")
- Das Auto wird schnell
- Er bleibt glücklich

### Adverbs

When adjectives function as adverbs, they take **no ending**:

- Er spricht schnell (not "schneller")
- Sie singt schön (not "schöne")

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Using the Wrong Declension

❌ Wrong: Der gut**er** Wein (definite article + strong ending)
✅ Right: Der gut**e** Wein (definite article + weak ending)

**Why:** After "der", use weak declension!

### Mistake 2: Forgetting Plural Changes

❌ Wrong: Die gut**e** Kinder
✅ Right: Die gut**en** Kinder

**Why:** In nominative/accusative plural, weak declension uses -en!

### Mistake 3: Adding Endings to Predicate Adjectives

❌ Wrong: Das Auto ist schnell**es**
✅ Right: Das Auto ist schnell

**Why:** Predicate adjectives (after "sein", "werden") take no ending!

### Mistake 4: Confusing Mixed vs Weak

❌ Wrong: Ein gut**e** Wein (indefinite article + weak ending)
✅ Right: Ein gut**er** Wein (indefinite article + mixed ending)

**Why:** After "ein", use mixed declension!

## Memory Tricks

### Trick 1: The "REN" Rule

For weak declension, almost everything ends in **-en**:
- Den gut**en** Wein
- Der gut**en** Weine
- Dem gut**en** Wein
- Des gut**en** Weines

**Exception:** Nominative singular (der gute, die gute, das gute)

### Trick 2: Strong = Stronger Endings

Strong declension has "stronger" (more varied) endings:
- -er, -e, -es, -e (nominative)
- -em, -er, -em, -en (dative)

### Trick 3: Mixed = Mix of Strong and Weak

Mixed declension uses strong endings in nominative masculine and neuter, but weak elsewhere:
- Ein gut**er** Wein (strong)
- Ein**en** gut**en** Wein (weak)

### Trick 4: Look at the Article

The article tells you everything:
- **No article** → Strong (adjective does the work)
- **Definite article** → Weak (article does the work)
- **Indefinite article** → Mixed (they share the work)

## Learning Tips

### Tip 1: Learn the Patterns, Not Individual Endings

Don't memorize every single ending. Learn the patterns:
- Strong: -er, -e, -es, -e (nom)
- Weak: almost everything -en
- Mixed: strong in nom. m/n, weak elsewhere

### Tip 2: Practice with Common Adjectives

Use high-frequency adjectives:
- gut (good)
- groß (big/tall)
- klein (small)
- schön (beautiful)
- neu (new)
- alt (old)
- jung (young)

### Tip 3: Start with Definite Article (Weak)

Start with weak declension because it's the easiest (mostly -en). Once comfortable, move to mixed and strong.

### Tip 4: Use Flashcards

Create flashcards with:
- Front: German phrase with blank
- Back: Complete phrase with correct ending

Example:
- Front: Der ___ Wein
- Back: Der gut**e** Wein

### Tip 5: Read and Listen

Expose yourself to German with correct adjective endings. Over time, you'll develop a feel for what sounds right.

## Summary of Key Rules

1. **Check what comes before the adjective**
   - Nothing → Strong
   - Der/die/das → Weak
   - Ein/mein/kein → Mixed

2. **Learn the weak declension first**
   - Almost everything ends in -en
   - Exception: nominative singular (-e)

3. **Remember predicate adjectives**
   - After "sein", "werden", "bleiben" → No ending

4. **Practice with common adjectives**
   - gut, groß, klein, schön, neu, alt

5. **Read and listen to German**
   - Develop a feel for correct endings

## Cultural Notes

### Adjective Endings in Formal German

In formal writing (business letters, academic papers), correct adjective endings are crucial. Mistakes stand out and can affect credibility.

### Regional Differences

Some dialects may use different adjective endings, but standard German (Hochdeutsch) follows the rules outlined in this guide.

### Adjective Endings in Spoken German

In casual conversation, Germans sometimes make mistakes with adjective endings, especially with less common words. However, high-frequency words are usually correct.

## Conclusion

German adjective endings may seem overwhelming, but they follow logical patterns:

1. **Learn the three types** — Strong, Weak, Mixed
2. **Check the article** — It tells you which declension to use
3. **Start with weak** — It's the easiest (mostly -en)
4. **Practice daily** — Use common adjectives in sentences
5. **Read German** — Develop a feel for correct endings
6. **Don't stress predicate adjectives** — They take no ending!
7. **Be patient** — It takes time to internalize the patterns

**Pro tip:** Focus on the most common situations first (definite article + weak declension, indefinite article + mixed declension). Master these, then gradually add the strong declension. Remember that even native speakers sometimes pause to think about adjective endings with less common words!
