# How to Make Friends in Germany: The Rules Nobody Tells You

You moved to Germany. You go to a bar. Nobody talks to you. You go to a party. People shake your hand and leave. You start thinking Germans are cold. They're not. You just don't know the rules yet.

## Rule 1: Friendship comes in categories

Germans separate people into strict categories:

- **Bekannter** — Acquaintance. Someone you know. You say hi, talk about the weather, move on.
- **Kollege** — Colleague. Work friend. You eat lunch together, complain about the boss, then go home.
- **Freund** — Friend. This is serious. A German Freund is someone they'd help move apartments at 7am on a Saturday. There are maybe 3-5 of these in a lifetime.

The jump from Bekannter to Freund takes months, sometimes years. This is not coldness. This is quality control.

## Rule 2: Small talk is not the entry point

In many cultures, small talk leads to friendship. In Germany, small talk is just noise. Germans don't become friends with someone because they chatted about the weather for 5 minutes.

**What works instead:** Shared activities. Join a **Verein** (club). Germans organize everything into clubs — running, cooking, board games, gardening, singing. This is where friendships actually start because you're doing something real together.

## Rule 3: When they invite you, they mean it

If a German says «Lass uns was machen» (let's do something), they actually want to see you. This is not politeness. This is a genuine invitation.

**Beware:** Do not cancel. In many cultures, canceling plans is normal. In Germany, if you cancel on a Freund, you just told them they're not important. If you say yes, show up.

## Rule 4: Punctuality is respect

If you agree to meet at 19:00, be there at 18:55. Being 10 minutes late without warning sends a clear message: your time is more important than theirs. Germans take this personally.

If you're running late, send a message. «Komme 10 Minuten später.» That's all it takes.

## Rule 5: Directness is honesty, not rudeness

Your German friend will tell you that your presentation was boring. They'll tell you that shirt doesn't look good. They'll tell you your cake is dry.

This is not an insult. In German culture, honesty IS friendship. A real friend tells you the truth. If they say your idea is good, they mean it — because they would have told you if it wasn't.

## Rule 6: The birthday rule

If it's your German friend's birthday, **you** call **them**. Not the other way around. In Germany, it's considered bad luck to congratulate someone before their actual birthday, and the birthday person should not have to organize their own celebration.

Learn this phrase: **«Alles Gute zum Geburtstag!»** — All the best for your birthday!

## How to start: Practical phrases

- **Lass uns mal was trinken gehen.** — Let's go grab a drink sometime. (The classic friendship opener.)
- **Hast du Lust auf ein Brettspielabend?** — Want to do a board game night?
- **Soll ich was mitbringen?** — Should I bring anything? (Always ask this when invited somewhere.)
- **Ich lade ein.** — It's on me. (Germans often split bills, so offering to pay is a genuine gesture.)
- **Danke für die Einladung.** — Thanks for the invitation.

## The truth

German friendships are hard to earn but nearly impossible to lose. Once a German calls you their Freund, you have someone who will drive across the country at 3am if your car breaks down. The wait is long. The reward is permanent.
