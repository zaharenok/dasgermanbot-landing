# German Supermarket Survival Guide: What Nobody Tells You About Einkaufen

German supermarkets look normal. They are not. The first time you shop in Germany, you will violate at least three unspoken rules. Here's what to expect and how to survive.

## Rule 1: Bag your own stuff. Fast.

Cashiers in Germany scan items at inhuman speed. They don't bag. They don't wait. Your groceries pile up at the end of the belt and you either keep up or cause a traffic jam.

Bring your own bag. Say **"Ich brauche keine Tüte"** (I don't need a bag) when asked, or **"Eine Tüte, bitte"** if you forgot yours. Bags cost money — usually 10-50 cents.

Pro tip: Put your bag open in the packing area before paying. You'll thank me.

## Rule 2: Cash is still king

Many smaller supermarkets and bakeries don't accept cards. The register will say **"Nur Bargeld"** (cash only). Larger chains (Rewe, Edeka) take cards, but always carry a €20 bill.

- **Kann ich mit Karte zahlen?** — Can I pay by card?
- **Nur Bargeld.** — Cash only.
- **Muss ich abwiegen?** — Do I need to weigh this?

Yes, some vegetables need weighing. Look for the scale in the produce section, press the button with the picture of your item, and stick the label on.

## Rule 3: The Pfand system

Germany charges a deposit on most bottles and cans. **Pfand** — €0.08 to €0.25 per container. You get it back when you return empties to the machine near the entrance.

- **Pfandwert:** Deposit value (printed on the label)
- **Einwegpfand:** Single-use deposit (€0.25)
- **Mehrwegpfand:** Reusable deposit (€0.08-€0.15)

The machine prints a receipt. Hand it to the cashier: **"Den Pfandbon, bitte."** The amount gets deducted from your total.

## Useful phrases

- **Wo finde ich...?** — Where do I find...?
- **Haben Sie...?** — Do you have...?
- **Ich möchte bitte bezahlen.** — I'd like to pay, please.
- **Kann ich mit dem Handy bezahlen?** — Can I pay with my phone?
- **Stimmt so.** — Keep the change. (At bakeries, not supermarkets — cashiers need exact change.)

## Reading the labels

German product labels are a language lesson:

- **Bio** — Organic (green sticker, usually)
- **Freilandhaltung** — Free range
- **Haltbar bis** — Best before
- **Zutaten** — Ingredients
- **Enthält** — Contains (allergens)

Watch out for **"light"** products — in Germany, "light" often means less fat, not low-calorie. And **"Frucht"** means fruit, but **"Fruchtgeschmack"** means fruit-flavored (zero actual fruit).

## The bakery counter

German bakeries are their own universe. You point, they slice. Essential words:

- **Ein Brötchen, bitte.** — One bread roll, please.
- **Geschnitten, bitte.** — Sliced, please.
- **Ein halbes Schwarzbrot.** — Half a loaf of black bread.
- **Sonst noch etwas?** — Anything else? (They always ask this.)
